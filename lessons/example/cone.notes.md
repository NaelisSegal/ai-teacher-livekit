# Why a cone is a third: teaching notes

An example of lesson prep: what a teacher knows besides the script (`cone.seg`).
A live teacher is given the script, these notes and the slides.

Time slot: 15 minutes. The script, with the class's working time, takes about 12.

## The aim

By the end, a student can find the volume of a cylinder and of a cone, knows why
the cone is a third, and can use that in a problem where water is poured from one
shape into another.

## How to teach it

Go slowly. Say every step of the arithmetic and write it on the board. Give the
class real time on the problem, then go through it a step at a time, asking for
each step. If nobody answers, that does not mean they followed: make the step
smaller and carry on at the same pace.

## Parts, and what can be cut

| Part | Title | Minutes | Must happen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Volume, and the can | 4 | Base times height; the can worked |
| 2 | The cone is a third | 5 | The video or the idea of it; the cone's formula |
| 3 | A problem | 5 | The class's own try; step by step |
| 4 | To remember | 1 | The two formulas; questions |

If time is short, cut the guess before the video, then the slide.

## Answers

- Can, r = 3, h = 10: 90 pi, about 282.7 cm cubed.
- The cone in it: 30 pi, about 94.2.
- The problem: the cup holds 24 pi; 9 pi h = 24 pi, so h = 8/3, about 2.67 cm.

## If the class is quick

- A cone holds 100 pi. The cylinder with the same base and height? 300 pi.
- A cone of radius 6 and slant height 10 (along the side): its height is 8 (6, 8, 10
  triangle), and its volume 96 pi.

## Mistakes to watch for

- Forgetting the third.
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Squaring after multiplying by pi wrongly: pi r squared is pi times (r times r).
